Can I be fired for participating in a legal psilocybin program?

For adults in Oregon who have accessed or are considering accessing the state’s licensed psilocybin program and want to understand whether their employer can take action against them for it.

The short answer

Oregon law prohibits most employers from disciplining or terminating employees solely for lawfully using psilocybin through the state’s licensed program — but the protection has real limits that matter. It does not apply to federal employers, employees in federally regulated industries, or workers under federal contracts. It does not protect someone who accesses psilocybin and then reports to work impaired. And it does not override an employer’s right to enforce drug-free workplace policies where those policies are federally required, or where a positive drug test result triggers consequences based on federal law rather than off-duty behavior. Understanding what the protection covers — and what it does not — is the most practically useful thing an employee can do before accessing the Oregon program.

Oregon’s employment protection

ORS 659A.315 prohibits employers from discriminating against an employee in the terms, conditions, or privileges of employment solely because the employee has lawfully used psilocybin pursuant to ORS 475A outside the workplace and during nonworking hours. An employer cannot fire, demote, discipline, or otherwise penalize an employee solely because they accessed Oregon’s licensed psilocybin program on their own time. This mirrors the structure of Oregon’s earlier cannabis employment protection, which operates under the same statute.

What the protection does not cover

The protection has four main limits.

Impairment at work is not protected. If an employee reports to work impaired by psilocybin — or if there is reasonable suspicion of impairment — the employer retains the full right to take action. Psilocybin’s acute effects can last six to eight hours, and residual effects including fatigue, altered perception, and emotional sensitivity can continue one to two days after a session. The timing decision before a session matters as much as the session itself.

Federally required drug-free workplace programs are not displaced by Oregon state law. Employers required by federal law to maintain drug-free workplaces — those receiving federal grants or contracts above specified thresholds under the Drug-Free Workplace Act of 1988, DOT-regulated employers conducting safety-sensitive work, and certain nuclear and defense contractors — operate under federal law under which psilocybin is a Schedule I controlled substance. Oregon’s employment protection cannot reach those federal compliance consequences.

Federal security clearance holders are subject to federal adjudication standards for drug use, not Oregon employment law. See Could a security clearance, federal job, or professional license be affected by psychedelic use? for that analysis.

Policy-based consequences that go beyond the use itself are not clearly foreclosed. An employer whose drug-free workplace policy prohibits use of all federally scheduled substances — not as a federal requirement, but as a voluntary company policy — may have room to take action even where ORS 659A.315 would otherwise protect the employee. The word “solely” in the statute means the employer cannot fire someone just because they lawfully used psilocybin. But if an employer’s policy prohibits involvement with federally scheduled substances and an employee violates it, the discharge may be defensible as based on policy violation rather than solely on use. No Oregon appellate decision has tested this line as of early 2026.

Drug testing and the detection window

Oregon law also restricts how employers can use drug test results. Under ORS 659A.315, an employer cannot use a positive drug test result alone as evidence of impairment at work for substances lawfully used under Oregon state law.

As a practical matter, standard employment drug test panels — including the SAMHSA-5 — do not include psilocybin or psilocin. Psilocin is detectable in urine for approximately 8–24 hours after ingestion, but only through a specifically targeted assay that is not part of routine screening. Most employees who access Oregon’s program will not produce a psilocybin-related positive test result under standard testing protocols. For DOT safety-sensitive positions and federally mandated testing programs, the analysis depends on what the specific panel includes and how results are interpreted under federal standards. See Drug testing and psychedelics: what employers can and cannot do for the full testing framework.

Safety-sensitive positions and vulnerable populations

Workers in safety-sensitive roles — healthcare, childcare, transportation, heavy equipment — occupy a category where the impairment standard carries heightened weight. Even where ORS 659A.315 would otherwise protect off-duty psilocybin use, an employer in one of these sectors has legitimate grounds to ensure workers are fit for duty. A nurse who accessed a psilocybin program and returned to patient care the following morning while still experiencing residual effects creates risk for patients that the employment protection statute was not designed to authorize. The practical guidance is the same as for all workers — plan adequate recovery time — but the margin for error is smaller and the conservative standard is two to three days before returning to safety-sensitive duties.

Federal employees

Oregon’s employment protection does not apply to federal government employees. The federal Drug-Free Workplace Act prohibits federal employees from using controlled substances — including Schedule I substances — whether on or off duty. A federal employee who accesses Oregon’s psilocybin program is not protected by ORS 659A.315 and faces potential federal employment consequences. See Could a security clearance, federal job, or professional license be affected by psychedelic use?.

Practical steps before accessing the program

An employee uncertain about their employer’s position should review their employment agreement and drug-free workplace policy before accessing psilocybin services — specifically whether the policy references federal law, federally scheduled substances, or participation in federally mandated testing programs. If it does, the state-law protection may not fully apply.

An employee should also confirm whether their position is safety-sensitive under federal regulations. DOT, FAA, FRA, FMCSA, FTA, and PHMSA each define safety-sensitive categories with specific drug testing obligations — a classification that means federal testing requirements apply regardless of Oregon’s employment protection.

And regardless of what the law allows, plan timing conservatively. A session scheduled with two to three days before the next shift, rather than returning to work the morning after, eliminates the impairment exposure that the statute does not protect against.

If adverse action has already occurred

An employee who has received adverse employment action they believe violates ORS 659A.315 may file a complaint with the Oregon Bureau of Labor and Industries. BOLI enforces ORS 659A.315 and investigates complaints of unlawful employment discrimination. The complaint deadline matters — consult an employment attorney promptly if adverse action has already occurred to ensure the filing window is not missed.

When public information may be enough

ORS 659A.315 is publicly available through the Oregon Legislative Assembly. BOLI publishes guidance on Oregon’s psilocybin employment protection at oregon.gov/boli. DOT drug testing regulations are at 49 CFR Part 40.

When you should speak with a lawyer

If you have received adverse employment action that you believe violates ORS 659A.315, consult an employment attorney before the BOLI complaint deadline runs. If you are in a federally regulated industry and uncertain whether mandatory drug testing overrides state law in your specific position, an employment attorney familiar with both federal and Oregon employment law can give you a clear answer.
