How Do I Find a Licensed Psychedelic Facilitator or Service Center?

Last reviewed: August 2026 · Psychedelic law changes quickly — verify current status before relying on this page.

Who this is for: Anyone ready to access psilocybin services in Oregon or Colorado and looking for practical guidance on how to locate a licensed provider, what to verify before booking, and what to watch out for.

The short answer

Finding a licensed provider in Oregon or Colorado begins with the state licensing directories — these are the authoritative sources for who holds a current license. Beyond the directories, service centers and facilitators maintain their own websites and accept inquiries directly. There is no central matching or referral service operated by either state. Verification matters: the existence of a business, a website, a training certificate, or a professional title does not confirm that a provider holds a current state license. Checking the directory before booking is the single most important step.

Oregon: the OPS Licensee Directory

The Oregon Health Authority maintains the OPS Licensee Directory — a publicly accessible list of licensed service centers, facilitators, manufacturers, and laboratories that have consented to have their contact information listed. Not all licensees elect to be listed, so the directory is not exhaustive, but it is the primary public-facing tool for locating licensed providers.

The directory is available through the OHA Oregon Psilocybin Services website at oregon.gov/psilocybin. It can be searched by license type and location. A provider listed in the directory holds a current license as of the date the directory reflects — but licenses are issued on a one-year term, and the directory’s currency depends on how recently it has been updated. If timing matters, contact OHA directly to confirm a specific license’s current status.

As of late 2025, approximately 30 licensed service centers were operating across Oregon, with around 360 licensed facilitators. Not all facilitators work at a single service center — some work across multiple locations or have their own affiliated practices within the licensed framework.

Colorado: DORA and DOR licensing information

Colorado’s licensing is split between two agencies, which means verification requires checking both depending on what you are looking for.

Facilitator licenses are issued by the Department of Regulatory Agencies (DORA) through the Office of Natural Medicine. DORA maintains licensing information and a lookup tool through its standard professional licensing database at dpo.colorado.gov. Healing center licenses are issued by the Department of Revenue (DOR) Natural Medicine Division. DOR maintains its own licensing database for natural medicine businesses.

Colorado’s licensed healing center program is in its early operational phase — the first regulated session occurred in June 2025, and the number of operational healing centers was limited as of early 2026. Checking directly with DORA and DOR for current licensee information is the most reliable approach while the program is still building out.

What to verify before booking

A current state license is the minimum verification step. Beyond that, several other things are worth confirming before committing to a service center or facilitator.

Is the facilitator working at a licensed service center or healing center? In Oregon, administration sessions must take place at a licensed facility. A licensed facilitator who offers to conduct sessions outside a licensed service center is not operating within the program’s authorized structure, regardless of their training or license status.

What are the service center’s procedures for safety incidents? Ask what happens if you have a difficult experience during a session, what protocols the service center has for medical emergencies, and how they handle post-session distress that requires clinical support beyond the facilitator’s scope.

Does the facilitator hold any additional credentials? As established in the article on facilitators and therapists, a facilitator license is distinct from a clinical credential. If you are bringing significant mental health history to your session, understanding whether your facilitator also holds a clinical license — and whether they are operating under both — is relevant to what protections apply to your engagement.

What does informed consent look like, and can you review documents before the preparation session? Some service centers make their intake forms and consent documents available in advance. Reviewing them before the preparation session allows you to arrive informed rather than first encountering them under time pressure.

What is the pricing structure? OPS does not regulate or set prices. Service centers and facilitators determine their own pricing. Understanding the full cost — product, facilitation, facility fee — before the preparation session avoids surprises. Some service centers offer sliding scale pricing as part of their social equity plan; it is worth asking if cost is a concern.

What to watch out for

Providers marketing sessions without mentioning a licensed service center. In Oregon, administration sessions must occur at a licensed service center. A provider who describes offering sessions at private homes, retreat properties, or other locations without reference to a licensed service center may not be operating within the licensed framework.

Certificate-based credential claims without a state license. Training certificates from psychedelic education programs are not the same as a state facilitator license. A provider may hold a certificate from a nationally recognized training program and still not hold a current Oregon or Colorado state license. Verify through the state directory, not through the training program’s graduate listing.

Out-of-state programs marketed to Oregon or Colorado residents. A growing number of retreat programs operate in jurisdictions — Jamaica, the Netherlands, Mexico — where psilocybin is not scheduled or where ceremonies are tolerated. These programs are not covered by Oregon’s or Colorado’s licensed framework and do not carry the regulatory protections of either state’s program. They may be appropriate options for some people, but they are a different category of access with different risk and recourse profiles.

Unlicensed integration providers presenting as part of a licensed service. Some service centers coordinate with outside integration coaches or practitioners as part of their service offering. If integration follow-up is provided by someone other than the licensed facilitator, it may not be covered by the state program’s regulatory framework. Understanding who provides each component of the service — and under what credential — matters.

If no licensed provider is accessible

For individuals outside Oregon and Colorado, or for those for whom the cost and logistics of the licensed programs are prohibitive, the legal options are more limited. Clinical trials remain the primary federally authorized pathway for psilocybin outside state programs. Trial listings are searchable at clinicaltrials.gov by condition, location, and eligibility. New Mexico’s Medical Psilocybin Act created a third state program, though it was still in its implementation phase as of early 2026 with enrollment not yet open.

Integration support from a licensed therapist familiar with psychedelic experiences is available in many areas without requiring access to a licensed session. A number of therapists provide integration services for clients who have had experiences in other contexts. The Multidisciplinary Association for Psychedelic Studies (MAPS) and similar organizations maintain therapist directories that can help identify clinicians with relevant training and experience.

Common mistakes and misconceptions

“A facilitator who was trained at a well-known program is licensed.” Training completion is not licensure. Check the state directory independently.

“The service center’s website says they’re licensed, so they are.” Self-reported license claims on a website should be verified against the actual state licensing directory.

“I can book directly without doing the preparation session first.” The preparation session is legally required before an administration session in both states. A service center cannot skip it, and a client cannot waive it.

When you should speak with a lawyer

You should speak with a lawyer if:

  • You participated in a session you believed was licensed and have reason to think it was not, and something went wrong
  • You are evaluating an out-of-state or international option and want to understand the legal differences before committing
