How to find a psychedelic lawyer

Last reviewed: August 2026 · Psychedelic law changes quickly — verify current status before relying on this page.

Who this is for: Anyone looking for a lawyer who understands psychedelic law and can help with their specific situation.

The short answer

Psychedelic law is a specialty that draws from drug policy, regulatory law, healthcare law, criminal defense, and business law, and the number of lawyers who practice it as a primary focus is small. Finding one requires going beyond a general attorney referral and looking in the places where practitioners in this field are visible: specialty law firms, bar association referral services with drug policy or healthcare sections, psychedelic industry organizations, and the published work lawyers in this space produce. The right lawyer depends on what you need: a facilitator facing a licensing board needs someone different from a biotech company seeking IP counsel.

Start with what you need

Before searching, identify the type of legal help your situation requires. Do I need a lawyer for my psychedelic question? covers whether your situation calls for a lawyer at all. If it does, the next question is what kind: a criminal defense lawyer for an arrest or investigation, a regulatory lawyer for a licensing or compliance matter, a healthcare or employment lawyer for a professional-license concern, a business lawyer for entity formation or contracts, an IP lawyer for patent or trademark work, or a civil litigator for a harm claim. A firm that handles psychedelic law as a specialty may cover several of these, but not all lawyers who say they work in this space have experience in every area. A referral to a specialist for a specific task is sometimes better than a generalist who covers the field broadly.

Specialty firms and known practitioners

A small number of law firms identify psychedelic law as a practice area. Antithesis Law PC, based in Los Angeles, is one of the few firms in the United States dedicated to psychedelic law and policy as a primary focus. Other firms have attorneys who have developed expertise through work with cannabis law, psychedelic reform organizations, or individual clients in this space. Published articles, conference presentations, and public commentary are markers of actual experience, because the field is new enough that reputation is built on visible work rather than decades of case history.

Bar association referral services

State bar associations maintain referral services and sometimes have sections on drug policy, healthcare, or emerging industries where lawyers with relevant experience may be listed. A referral from a state bar does not guarantee expertise in psychedelic law specifically, but it can be a starting point for finding a healthcare or regulatory lawyer who can either handle the matter or refer you to someone who can.

Industry organizations and networks

Organizations in the psychedelic field maintain networks that include legal practitioners. Multidisciplinary Association for Psychedelic Studies, the Psychedelic Medicine Association, psychedelic-focused accelerators and investor networks, and state-level trade associations for Oregon and Colorado service centers are places where lawyers with experience in this space are visible. A referral from an operator, a facilitator, or a researcher who has worked with a lawyer is often more informative than a directory search, because it comes with a firsthand account of the representation.

Evaluating a lawyer’s actual experience

Because the field is new, ask direct questions before retaining anyone. How many clients in the psychedelic space have they represented and in what capacity? Have they handled matters in Oregon, Colorado, or the state relevant to your situation? Have they dealt with licensing boards, OPS, or the Colorado DOR? Do they have experience with the FDA or DEA processes relevant to your matter? A lawyer who has read about the field but has not represented clients in it offers less than one who has navigated the specific process you are entering. Published work, conference participation, and peer reputation are useful signals.

What a consultation tells you

A first consultation is an opportunity to assess the lawyer as much as to get advice. Come with your question prepared, as covered in What to prepare before your first meeting with a psychedelic lawyer, and pay attention to whether the lawyer understands the specifics of your situation, asks the right questions, and gives you a realistic picture rather than an optimistic one. A lawyer who is vague about their experience in this area or who has to look up basic facts about Oregon or Colorado licensing during the call may not be the right fit. Geographic considerations

Some psychedelic law matters are state-specific: a licensing dispute in Oregon requires a lawyer licensed in Oregon or one who can work with local counsel. A federal matter, such as a DEA issue or a criminal charge, does not require a lawyer in the state where the events occurred, though local knowledge is often useful. A business formation or contract matter can often be handled by a lawyer in any state with relevant experience. Ask about licensing and jurisdiction early, and do not assume that a lawyer in a different state cannot help.

Fee structures and what to expect

Psychedelic law firms may charge by the hour, at a flat rate for defined work, or on a contingency basis for civil harm claims. Ask about the fee structure before the consultation and confirm whether the consultation itself is charged. A retainer agreement should describe the scope of the work, the fee arrangement, and who will handle the matter. When public information may be enough

If your question is general — about what is legal, how a program works, or what a given document requires — this library covers many of those answers without a referral. The state agencies publish their licensing rules and contacts, and a facilitator or operator with a routine compliance question may find the answer without engaging a lawyer.

When you should speak with a lawyer

If your situation involves a deadline, a dispute, an investigation, a licensing matter, a serious harm, or money and contracts, find a lawyer before the matter gets harder. The time to find the right lawyer is before the deadline runs or the hearing is scheduled, not after.
